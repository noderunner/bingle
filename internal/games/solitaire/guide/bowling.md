# Bowling Solitaire

Sid Sackson's game from *A Gamut of Games* (1969). Twenty cards make ten frames of bowling, and every frame is a little puzzle in adding up. The tutorial under **Learn to play** shows it being played.

## The cards

Ace to 10 of two suits, twenty cards. Suits do not matter, and an ace counts 1. For each frame all twenty are shuffled and set out:

* **The pins**, ten cards face up in a triangle: a b c d at the back, e f g, h i, and j at the front.
* **The balls**, ten cards face down in three piles of 5, 3 and 2, the top card of each turned up.

## A play

Use one face-up ball card to knock down:

* **one pin**, if its value ends in the same digit as the ball, or
* **two or three pins that touch**, if the last digit of their sum does.

So a 6 can take a 6, or a 2 and a 4, or a 7 and a 9 (16), or a 1, a 2 and a 3. A 10 counts as 0.

The ball card and the pins leave the table, and the next card of that pile turns up. Cards out of play stay in view, so you can count what is left.

## What you may knock down

* **The first play on a rack** cannot touch the back row (a, b, c, d) or take the centre pin (f) alone.
* **Every later play** must include a pin next to one already down.

## The two balls

The **first ball** lasts until no play is left, or until you choose **End this ball**. All ten pins gone is a **strike**.

Otherwise the top card of each ball pile is thrown away, the next is turned up, and the **second ball** goes on against the pins still standing. Clearing them now is a **spare**. Ending a ball early can be right: it trades a poor play now for a better set of balls.

## Scoring

As in ten-pin bowling: a frame scores the pins it knocked down, a spare adds the next ball, a strike the next two. A spare in the tenth frame earns one more ball and a strike two, on a new rack. A perfect game is 300.

## Playing here

Click the pins you want to knock down, in any order; the balls that can do it light up. Then click the ball.
